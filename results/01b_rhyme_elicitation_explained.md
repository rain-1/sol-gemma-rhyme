# Trying to make Pythia-410M rhyme more reliably

## Outcome

The best general prompt increased greedy exact-rhyme completion from **7 of 25
(28%) to 10 of 25 (40%)**. It placed an exact rhyme among the model's ten leading
choices for **20 of 25 poems (80%)**, up from 17 of 25 (68%).

That is a useful improvement—a 43% relative increase in greedy successes—but it
is not yet a reliably rhyming model. Repeating the demonstration up to 30
couplets did not move greedy accuracy above 40%.

The ten successful longer-context prompts are now a 100%-positive working set
for interpretability. This does **not** mean we discovered a prompt with 100%
accuracy: the working set contains the ten successes selected after evaluating
all 25. It is suitable for asking what happens inside Pythia when it does rhyme,
not for reporting generalization accuracy.

## The strongest prompt

Before each target couplet we supplied three completed rhyming couplets:

```text
The rain made mirrors in the lane
At dusk there came a distant train
The fire faded to a glow
Beyond the glass descended snow
A gull went wheeling by the sea
It spread its wings and wandered free
The roof began to drum with rain
Beyond the fields there passed a
```

For this example the greedy completion changed from the non-rhyme `line` to the
exact rhyme `train`.

Across all 25 targets, the successful anchors in this condition were:

```text
rain, flame, stream, bell, star, sea, day, door, ground, deep
```

## Does the demonstrated rhyming pattern cause the improvement?

Not clearly. We compared four versions of exactly the same target poems:

| Prefix condition | Greedy rhyme | Rhyme in top 10 | Mean rhyme probability |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No preceding lines | 28% | 68% | 12.99% |
| Three rhyming couplets | 40% | 80% | 17.73% |
| Same six lines shuffled so adjacent lines do not rhyme | 36% | 80% | 18.03% |
| Same three pairs with line order reversed | 32% | 76% | 16.90% |

The shuffled control performs almost as well as the rhyming context and has
slightly *more* total probability on rhyme words. Therefore we cannot claim that
Pythia inferred the demonstrated `AABBCC` scheme. Much of the improvement seems
to come from establishing a longer, poem-like context.

## Other things we tried

- One through thirty preceding completed couplets: greedy accuracy plateaued at
  40% after three couplets.
- Labels and instructions such as `A Rhyming Poem`: no reliable improvement.
- Lists of rhyme pairs such as `cat, hat`: substantially worse; the base model
  tended to repeat the anchor or copy an earlier example.
- Five coherent eight-line `AABBCCDD` poems generated by Claude Haiku: only one
  greedily rhymed. Longer context alone did not solve ambiguous final lines.
- Rewriting the original couplets to make the intended completion more natural:
  top-10 coverage improved to 92%, but greedy rhyme stayed at 32% because normal
  semantic completions still competed with rhyme.
- The non-deduplicated `EleutherAI/pythia-410m` checkpoint: 24% plain and 36%
  with three demonstrations, slightly worse than the deduplicated model.

## Follow-up: making every preceding poem distinct

The original length sweep recycled a bank of three to six couplets. We therefore
used Claude Haiku to create 24 unique, self-contained couplets. Every pair was
checked against CMUdict to confirm that its final words rhyme exactly.

For each target poem, we also removed any demonstration whose rhyme family
overlapped the target anchor. For example, a demonstration ending in `play` and
`way` is excluded when the target anchor is `day`. This prevents a larger prompt
from improving merely because it supplies candidate answers from the target's
rhyme family.

We evaluated increasingly long prefixes and a control made by shuffling the
second lines of exactly the same demonstrations:

| Distinct preceding couplets | Greedy rhyme | Shuffled control | Rhyme in top 10 | Shuffled top 10 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 28% | 28% | 64% | 64% |
| 3 | 28% | 32% | 72% | 72% |
| 5 | 28% | 32% | 72% | 72% |
| 10 | 32% | 32% | 68% | 72% |
| 15 | 28% | 32% | 72% | 72% |
| 20 | 28% | 32% | 68% | 68% |

Distinct poems did **not** improve the greedy rhyme rate. The best distinct
condition reached 32%, below the 40% obtained from the smaller repeated context,
and the shuffled controls were just as good or better. More demonstrations are
therefore not teaching this 410M base model a general rhyme rule under the current
task design.

This makes the 40% repeated-context result less interesting as evidence of
in-context rhyme learning. It likely includes lexical/style priming from those
particular repeated lines. The scientifically useful next move is not an even
longer prefix; it is to identify prompt pairs where changing only the rhyme
anchor causally changes the completion distribution.

## What this means for interpretability

We now have ten robust positive examples under one fixed prompt and fifteen
failures under the same prompt. That supports two useful comparisons:

1. successful versus unsuccessful poems, to nominate components associated with
   rhyme behavior; and
2. each successful poem versus an exact counterfactual where only its rhyme
   anchor changes, to test whether the nominated component actually carries
   information about the anchor.

The second comparison is essential. Several final words are strongly suggested
by sentence meaning, so a rhyming output is not automatically evidence that the
model consulted the earlier line ending.

## Reproduce the comparison

```bash
.venv/bin/rhyme-interp elicit
.venv/bin/rhyme-interp distinct
```

This writes all 100 rows—25 targets under four conditions—to
`artifacts/elicitation.jsonl`.

The distinct-poem experiment writes 300 rows to
`artifacts/distinct_elicitation.jsonl`.
